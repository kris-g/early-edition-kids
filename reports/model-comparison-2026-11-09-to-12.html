Do the Monday findings hold?

Short answer

Partly. Monday's ranking at the bottom holds: GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro made up details and broke the length limits far too often. The top does not hold. On Monday Sonnet 5.5 looked as accurate as Opus 5.5. Over four days Opus was clearly ahead: 21 of 24 stories were clean first time, against 15 of 24 for Sonnet.

What stayed the same

  • Opus was the most reliable writer every day: 5, 5, 6 and 5 of 6 stories clean first time. In 24 stories the checker found invented details only twice. On Monday it gave a cause the article didn't give, in the Mars story. On Tuesday it rewrote the Liverpool player ratings article it had handled cleanly on Monday and added a place (Big saves and great goals in a win in London, when the article never says London).
  • GLM 5.3 came last on all four days. Only 3 of 24 stories were clean. It broke the title, teaser or word-count limits 13 times, and it got facts wrong. It reported a 3-0 win in a match where Palace had scored. It also used shooting in a football story, which the blocklist rejects.
  • DeepSeek V4 Pro stayed in third place (8 of 24 clean). Its teasers were often too long, up to 76 characters. It also put words in someone's mouth: There is nothing higher than the Champions League appears as a direct quote, but the manager's real sentence was longer and different.
  • Nothing unsafe. No model refused anything, and the checker marked every story safe.
  • The Opus checker stayed sharp. It caught invented dates, numbers made by adding other numbers (six kelp beds when the article lists two, two and two), reworded quotes and overstated claims.

What changed

  • Sonnet slipped after Monday. It was clean on 3 or 4 of 6 stories each day, and the checker found invented details in 6 of its 24 stories. It wrote filmed six kelp beds on two different days, gave a date the article never states (12 November), and made one plain science mistake: Carbon is a gas that adds to climate change. It also drifted from house style, with NASA and Title Case headlines such as Seals Have a Hearing Superpower.
  • DeepSeek was slower on later days. It averaged about 20 seconds a story overall, against 4 for Sonnet and 11 for Opus.

How much to trust this

  • Candidates per day: 6 on Monday, 16 on Tuesday, 19 on Wednesday and 24 on Thursday. Monday's small pool came from how far back the feeds reach, so its picks overlap heavily.
  • Sample size: 24 stories per model, one attempt each. In real runs a failed story gets one rewrite with the reasons, so the "clean first time" gaps overstate how many stories would finally be lost. They do show how often each model needs correcting.
  • Repeated articles: the comparison runs keep no story history and the 48-hour feed windows overlap, so some articles came up on more than one day (Salah, the Premier League table, the T. rex footprints, the seals, the kelp robot). That is useful here, because it shows the same model writing the same article twice. It would not happen in production, where stories used in the last 14 days are skipped.
